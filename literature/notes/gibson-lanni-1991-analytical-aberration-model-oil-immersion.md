## Why This Matters

This is the primary source for depth-dependent and design-condition-dependent aberration of a high-NA oil-immersion objective, and it shows aberration directly in measured through-focus PSF stacks. It is the best of the four papers for the thesis claim that aberrations arise from the sample and its environment as well as from the optics and degrade high-NA imaging. It is not a source about deconvolution cost or noise.

## Relevance to the thesis

- Supports: G24 strongly for the specimen-and-environment route (refractive-index mismatch, specimen depth, coverslip, oil, detector position) and for the effect on high-NA imaging (pp. 1601-1604, 1610-1611). G30 partly: the PSF is a through-focus stack and measured through-focus bead stacks show aberration as asymmetry about the in-focus plane (pp. 1605, 1608-1610), but defocus images are used only to test a forward model, not to estimate aberrations. G25 only weakly: an accurate PSF is needed for 3-D reconstruction (p. 1601) and the model could improve reconstructions (p. 1612).
- Does not support: G25 claims about cost, slowness, artifacts or noise amplification (not discussed); G26 and G27 (no wavefront sensing, adaptive optics or deformable mirrors; the only remedy mentioned is a re-designed objective and index-matched immersion, p. 1613); anything about lateral shifts between views or spatially varying aberration across the field (the model is for an on-axis point source, and radial variation is set aside, p. 1612); any statement about Fourier light-field microscopy.

## Differences to state if cited

- The aberration treated is mainly axially symmetric spherical aberration from index mismatch and depth, in a single-pupil wide-field microscope with an oil-immersion objective; the thesis works with views from a lenslet array in Fourier space and models aberration as shifts between views.
- The model is for an on-axis point source with radial shift variance neglected over a roughly 100 micrometre field; it is not a model of spatially varying aberration.
- Fluorescent beads under a coverslip or in agarose gel, not tissue; no scattering is modelled. The authors note the excitation beam in confocal operation is also aberrated and was not modelled (p. 1605-1607).
- The measured system's true design detector distance was not known (Fig. 12 caption, p. 1611), while the computed PSFs assume 160 mm.

## Safe Wording

Aberration in high-NA oil-immersion imaging arises when the specimen, coverslip, immersion oil or detector position depart from the objective's design conditions, and it is most severe in thick specimens whose refractive index differs from that of the oil (Gibson and Lanni 1991, pp. 1601 and 1604).
