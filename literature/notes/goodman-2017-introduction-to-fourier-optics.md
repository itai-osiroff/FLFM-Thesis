## Citation

Joseph W. Goodman, *Introduction to Fourier Optics*, 4th edition, W. H. Freeman
and Company (Macmillan Learning), New York, 2017. ISBN `978-1-319-11916-4`
(front matter of the local PDF, `literature/Joseph W. Goodman - Introduction to
Fourier Optics (2017, W. H. Freeman) - libgen.li.pdf`, an OCR scan).

## Passages checked (2026-11-12, OCR text; page numbers not verified)

- Section 7.1.3, "Polychromatic Illumination: The Coherent and Incoherent
  Cases" (book pages about 189-192). Defines spatially incoherent illumination
  as phasor amplitudes at object points varying in totally uncorrelated ways,
  and shows that for incoherent light the image intensity is the convolution of
  the intensity impulse response with the ideal image intensity: "an incoherent
  imaging system is linear in intensity, rather than amplitude."
- Section 7.6.2, "Incoherent Case" (confocal microscopy, about book page 223):
  if the object is fluorescent, "the incident light intensity stimulates the
  object to emit incoherent light, generally at a different wavelength than that
  of the illumination."
- Section 8.4.5, "Incoherent Structured Illumination Imaging": fluorescent
  objects "emit incoherent light in response to an illumination intensity,
  where the illumination of the object can be coherent or incoherent."

## Thesis use

Supports the Chapter 1 sentence that fluorescence emission is incoherent and
adds in intensity, independent of whether the excitation is coherent (laser) or
not. The book states incoherence of fluorescent emission; the "fluorophores
radiate independently" gloss is the standard physical reason and is not
quoted from these sections. Not read beyond the passages above.
