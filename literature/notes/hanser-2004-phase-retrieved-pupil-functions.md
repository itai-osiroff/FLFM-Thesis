## Why This Matters

This is a primary source for the claim that a through-focus series of a point source (a bead) can be used to recover the pupil function, and hence the aberrations, of a wide-field microscope, and for the statement that deconvolution needs an accurate PSF. It is a computational (software) route to aberration characterisation, so it is a counterpoint to the thesis's own method, which does not estimate a PSF or a pupil.

## Relevance to the thesis

- Supports: G30, directly: a defocus (through-focus) series of a bead is used to recover the pupil function and thereby aberrations (p. 35, Fig. 2), and the paper explicitly uses a series of sections "with different defocus" (p. 34). G25 in part: deconvolution needs an accurate PSF; theoretical or rotationally averaged PSFs give artefacts; measured PSFs carry noise and cannot be modified (pp. 32-33, 46). G24 weakly: sample-induced and index-mismatch aberrations are a practical problem in biological imaging (p. 32, p. 43, p. 47). G26 only indirectly: the Introduction notes that direct pupil measurement requires extra hardware.
- Does not support: that software solutions amplify noise (not stated; the paper says phase retrieval reduces PSF noise), that they are slow or costly (not stated; PSF calculation is described as fast), anything about deformable mirrors or adaptive optics hardware (not stated), anything about spatially varying correction across the field (not tested), anything about Fourier or light-field microscopy, and the claim that aberrations appear as shifts between views.

## Differences to state if cited

- It is wide-field microscopy with a 3D (z-stack) PSF and a single bead, not light-field microscopy, and not a 2D scene.
- Its through-focus images are used to retrieve a pupil function for later PSF calculation and deconvolution, the opposite of the thesis, which uses no PSF, no pupil and no deconvolution.
- Its examples are index-mismatch spherical aberration, a phase ring and an oil drop, not general sample aberrations in a biological specimen.
- It reports that a retrieved-pupil PSF matches measured PSFs only after an empirical OTF rescaling, so the model is imperfect.
- It is not a source for the Shack-Hartmann or adaptive-optics claims.

## Safe Wording

A through-focus series of a subresolution fluorescent bead can be used to estimate the pupil function of a wide-field microscope by iterative phase retrieval, and the PSF calculated from that pupil performs like a measured PSF in deconvolution, whereas rotationally averaged or purely theoretical PSFs produce artefacts (Hanser et al., 2004, pp. 35, 46).
