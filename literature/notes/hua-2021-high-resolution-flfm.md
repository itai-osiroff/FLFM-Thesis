# Hua 2021 - High-Resolution Fourier Light-Field Microscopy

## Citation

Xuanwen Hua, Wenhao Liu, and Shu Jia, "High-resolution Fourier light-field
microscopy for volumetric multi-color live-cell imaging," *Optica*, 8(5),
614-620, 2021. DOI: `10.1364/OPTICA.419236`.

Primary source: <https://doi.org/10.1364/OPTICA.419236>

## Why This Matters

This paper is a strong follow-on FLFM source showing that FLFM continued into
higher-resolution live-cell volumetric imaging. It supports the thesis'
motivation that FLFM is an active snapshot volumetric microscopy family, while
also helping distinguish that family from the thesis' narrower 2D bead-scene
rectification problem.

## Key Claims

- HR-FLFM is presented for fast volumetric live-cell imaging.
- The paper reports near-diffraction-limited resolution in three dimensions,
  extended focal depth, and millisecond-scale scanning-free volume acquisition.
- The source is useful for motivation and lineage, but its reconstruction goals
  differ from this thesis' warp-and-average rectification method.

## Useful Equations, Figures, Or Methods

- Use as a modern FLFM application paper when motivating why FLFM matters for
  high-speed microscopy.
- Use to frame the high-resolution end of the FLFM literature without importing
  its claims into the local method.

## Possible Thesis Use

- A concise citation that FLFM is used for fast volumetric live-cell imaging.
- Context for why this thesis should be careful not to claim volumetric
  recovery when it evaluates 2D fluorescent bead scenes.

## Safe Wording

Hua et al. extend FLFM toward high-resolution, scanning-free volumetric
live-cell imaging. This thesis cites the work as evidence of the FLFM
literature's broader volumetric direction, while keeping the present
contribution bounded to rectification of elemental views for 2D bead scenes.

## Caveats

- Do not use this source to claim that the current setup reaches
  near-diffraction-limited 3D resolution.
- Do not use it as validation for tissue-like scattering or the thesis' local
  algorithm.
