# Jacques 2013 - Optical Properties Of Biological Tissues

## Citation

Steven L. Jacques, "Optical properties of biological tissues: a review,"
*Physics in Medicine and Biology*, 58(11), R37-R61, 2013.
DOI: `10.1088/0031-9155/58/11/R37`.

Primary source: <https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9155/58/11/R37>

## Why This Matters

This is the main tissue-optics review to pair with Goodman. Goodman provides
statistical-optics theory; Jacques provides biological-tissue optical-property
language: absorption, scattering, anisotropy, reduced scattering, refractive
index, wavelength dependence, and tissue variability.

## Key Claims

- Tissue optical properties affect diagnostic and therapeutic light-based
  applications and are needed before choosing a light-transport model.
- Relevant tissue properties include absorption coefficient, scattering
  coefficient, scattering phase function, anisotropy, reduced scattering, and
  refractive index.
- Tissue values vary by composition, person, location, time, and wavelength,
  so tabulated numbers must be used carefully.

## Useful Equations, Figures, Or Methods

- Reduced scattering coefficient:

```text
mu_s' = mu_s * (1 - g)
```

- Use for biological tissue language, not for a full forward model of the
  thesis' chicken-breast or eyedrop media.

## Safe Wording

Jacques reviews biological tissue optical properties and emphasizes absorption,
scattering, anisotropy, reduced scattering, refractive index, wavelength
dependence, and variability. This thesis can use the review to explain why
tissue-like media introduce degradation and why results from selected media
should not be generalized without care.

## Caveats

- The review is not specific to FLFM.
- It does not provide measured properties for the thesis' exact chicken-breast
  and eyedrop preparations unless those are separately measured or cited.
