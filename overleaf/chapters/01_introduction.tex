\chapter{Introduction}
Non-invasive biological imaging have made leaps in the 20th and 21st centuries in resolution, speed and sensitivity. Some of the most powerful examples include magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), ultrasound imaging, and CT scans. These technologies have become pillars of modern diagnostic medicine, allowing clinicians to see into the human body, even in real-time, without the need for invasive procedures, revolutionizing patient care, and early detection. Asid from medical application, research fields such as neuroscience, biology, and materialss science have also benefited from the ability to inspect deep structures and fast processes in-vivo.

Along with the above mentioned techniques, optics-based methods have also emerged as useful supplamentary tools, mostly for ex-vivo and in-vitro applications.
