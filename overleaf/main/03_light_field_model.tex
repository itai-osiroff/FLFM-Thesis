\chapter{Light Field Microscopy Model}

In this chapter a physical model for the image formation within our physical system will be presented (in congruency with the model presented in \cite{guo2019fourierLightField} with slight deviations to accommodate our specific setup).
The model will be used to describe the formed images, and validate the systems output, characteristics, and limitations.
It is important to note that the model is a continuous space model, and the sensor, and simulation, outputs are discrete and therefore will contain sampling steps.
The specifics describing the physical traits of the optical system are discussed in \cref{ch:experimental-setup}.

The physical system is a Fourier light-field fluorescence microscope, meaning that the specimen imaged is excited through a laser beam at 488nm, the microscope collects the emitted ~532nm fluorescent light through a high-NA objective, relayed through a microlens array placed at the plane conjugated to the back pupil plane of the objective, and relayed again to the camera sensor.
The relays here are simple 4F systems: the first designed to magnify said conjugated plane x3 to fit the microlens array, and the second to demagnify x0.375 the light-field captured by the microlens array to fit in the camera sensor area.
These specific magnifications were chosen to maximize the amount of light field sub-aperture images within the sensor area, while also ensuring that they don't overlap.
The specific lens details of the relays, microlens array, and other optical and sensor hardware components are discussed in \cref{ch:experimental-setup} as well.

In order to orient the image-formation mode, the $x$ and $y$ axes are defined to be parallel to the specimen and sensor planes and oriented to be also parallel to the grid lines of the microlens array, while the $z$ axis is defined to be perpendicular to them and directed away from the specimen and towards main camera sensor (which means that the validation camera sensor is placed at $z$<0).
While describing the conjugated plane of the objective, the $u$ and $v$ axes are used, and are parallel to the $x$ and $y$ axes due to the Fourier transform direction-preserving quality.
