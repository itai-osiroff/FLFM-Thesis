\section{Sampling of the views}
\label{sec:app-sampling}

Let $P$ be the camera pixel pitch, $N_{px}$ the number of pixels across the sensor, and $D_{cam}=N_{px}P$ its width. The objective and tube lens image the specimen with magnification $M$, each lenslet reimages that image onto its focal plane with magnification $f_{MLA}/f_{FL}$, and the camera relay adds $M_{cam}$. A camera pixel therefore covers $p$ in object space, and the sampling ratio is the lateral resolution of a view in units of $p$:
\begin{equation}
    p = \frac{P}{M_T}, \qquad
    M_T = M\,\frac{f_{MLA}}{f_{FL}}\,M_{cam}, \qquad
    S_r = \frac{\Delta x_{LF}}{p}.
    \label{eq:app-sampling-def}
\end{equation}
Resolving $\Delta x_{LF}$ needs at least two pixels across it, so Nyquist sampling is $S_r\ge2$ \cite{guo2019fourierLightField}.

Two fractions measure how much of the available area the image uses. The filled fraction $\kappa$ is the share of the maximal field of view $FOV_{max}$ that the field uses, where $FOV_{max}$ is the field at which Eq.~\eqref{eq:fov-mla-f}, carried to the full path ($f_{obj}\to f_{FL}/M$), holds with equality. The sensor fill $\eta$ is the share of the sensor width covered by the pupil image:
\begin{equation}
    FOV_{max} = \frac{d_{MLA}\,f_{FL}}{f_{MLA}\,M}, \qquad
    \kappa = \frac{FOV}{FOV_{max}} \le 1, \qquad
    \eta = \frac{M_{cam}\,D_{proj}}{D_{cam}} \le 1.
    \label{eq:app-fractions}
\end{equation}
The first holds because the views must not overlap (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fov-mla-f}), and the second because the pupil image must fit on the sensor. The part of the lenslet pitch on the sensor that the image of the field leaves empty is a guard band.

To obtain $S_r$ from them, note that the definitions of $\kappa$ and $FOV_{max}$ give $FOV\,M\,f_{MLA}/f_{FL}=\kappa\,d_{MLA}$, so the field spans $\kappa\,d_{MLA}M_{cam}$ on the camera. With $M_{cam}=\eta D_{cam}/D_{proj}$ and $D_{cam}=N_{px}P$, it covers $N_{field}=\kappa\,\eta\,N_{px}\,d_{MLA}/D_{proj}$ pixels. The field is $FOV=N_{field}\,p$ wide, and $D_{proj}/d_{MLA}=NA/NA_{view}=\Delta x_{LF}/\Delta x_o$, so
\begin{equation}
    S_r = \frac{\Delta x_{LF}\,N_{field}}{FOV}
        = \eta\,\kappa\,\frac{N_{px}\,\Delta x_o}{FOV}
        \le \frac{N_{px}\,\Delta x_o}{FOV}.
    \label{eq:app-sampling}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is the sampling of a conventional microscope that images the same field onto the same camera, and equality holds when the field equals $FOV_{max}$ and the pupil image fills the sensor. Neither $N$ nor $NA_{view}$ appears in it: the light field keeps the pixel count of the conventional microscope and divides it among the views, each view coarser in resolution and in pixel count by the same factor.

\paragraph{Undersampling.} If $S_r<2$ the pixel, not the optics, limits the lateral resolution, which becomes about $2p$ instead of $\Delta x_{LF}$, a factor $2/S_r$ coarser. The axial resolution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:axial-lf} is computed from the lateral one, so it worsens by the same factor.

\section{Depth of field}
\label{sec:app-dof}

The depth of field of a view is the smaller of two limits, the blur of the view itself and the cross-talk between neighboring views \cite{guo2019fourierLightField}.

\paragraph{Diffraction and pixel blur.} A view has aperture $NA_{view}$, so an emitter at defocus $z$ is blurred over a diameter of about $2\,z\,NA_{view}$ in object space. The blur reaches the diffraction distance $\Delta x_{LF}=\lambda/(2NA_{view})$ at $|z|=\lambda/(4NA_{view}^2)$ and the projected pixel $p$ at $|z|=p/(2NA_{view})$. Taking both sides of focus and adding the circle-of-confusion term to the diffraction term, as in the classical expression for a microscope, gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}^{px}
    = \frac{\lambda}{2\,NA_{view}^2} + \frac{p}{NA_{view}}
    = \mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}\left(1+\frac{1}{S_r}\right),
    \label{eq:app-dof-pixel}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof-lf} and the last step uses $p=\Delta x_{LF}/S_r$ and $\mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}=\Delta x_{LF}/NA_{view}$. The pixel term is the fraction $1/(S_r+1)$ of the total.

Guo's wave term is the full width at half maximum, twice the width used here, so in that convention the bracket is $(2+1/S_r)$ and the pixel share is $1/(2S_r+1)$:
\begin{equation}
    \text{pixel share} = \frac{1}{S_r+1}\ \ (\text{this thesis}), \qquad
    \frac{1}{2S_r+1}\ \ (\text{Guo}).
    \label{eq:app-dof-share}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Cross-talk.} An emitter off the focal plane shifts laterally in each off-axis view by the parallax of Section~\ref{sec:factors-of-n}. A view records only a window of the image formed by the objective and tube lens, of width $M\,FOV_{max}=d_{MLA}f_{FL}/f_{MLA}$, and an emitter in focus sits at its center, so it can shift by $M\,FOV_{max}/2$ there before its light enters the neighboring window. Following Guo we take the nearest lenslet, which views the emitter at $\tan\theta_1=d_{MLA}/f_{FL}$, and project the allowed range back to object space with the axial magnification $M^2$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Range}_{z}^{xt}
    = \frac{1}{M^2}\,\frac{M\,FOV_{max}}{\tan\theta_1}
    = \frac{f_{FL}^2}{M^2\,f_{MLA}}.
    \label{eq:app-dof-crosstalk}
\end{equation}
This is purely geometric: the pitch cancels, and neither the wavelength nor the pixel size enters. For our design values ($f_{FL}=750$~mm, $f_{MLA}=55$~mm, $M=50$) it is about 4~mm, far above Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-dof-pixel}, so the cross-talk limit does not bind.
