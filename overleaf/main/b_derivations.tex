\section{Sampling of the views}
\label{sec:app-sampling}

On the idealized path of Figure~\ref{fig:flfm-path}, the sensor lies in the focal plane of the lenslets, and each view receives $N_{px}/N$ of the pixels across the sensor (\cref{sec:fov-sensor-spec}). On the full path of Figure~\ref{fig:flfm-full-path}, the pupil image need not match the sensor, and the camera relay fits one to the other. Let $P$ be the camera pixel pitch, $N_{px}$ the number of pixels across the sensor, and $D_{cam}=N_{px}P$ its width. The objective and tube lens image the specimen with magnification $M$, each lenslet reimages that image onto its focal plane with magnification $f_{MLA}/f_{FL}$, and the camera relay adds $M_{cam}$. A camera pixel therefore covers $p$ in object space, and the sampling ratio $\alpha$ is the lateral resolution of a view in units of $p$:
\begin{equation}
    p = \frac{P}{M_T}, \qquad
    M_T = M\,\frac{f_{MLA}}{f_{FL}}\,M_{cam}, \qquad
    \alpha = \frac{\Delta x_{LF}}{p}.
    \label{eq:app-sampling-def}
\end{equation}
Resolving $\Delta x_{LF}$ needs at least two pixels across it, so Nyquist sampling is $\alpha\ge2$ \cite{guo2019fourierLightField}. Chapter~\ref{ch:light-field-microscope} targets $1\le\alpha\le2$, because the views are already coarse.

Two fractions measure how much of the available area the image uses. The filled fraction $\kappa$ is the share of the maximal field of view $FOV_{max}$ that the field uses, where $FOV_{max}$ is the field at which Eq.~\eqref{eq:fov-mla-f}, carried to the full path ($f_{obj}\to f_{FL}/M$), holds with equality. The sensor fill $\eta$ is the share of the sensor width covered by the pupil image:
\begin{equation}
    FOV_{max} = \frac{d_{MLA}\,f_{FL}}{f_{MLA}\,M}, \qquad
    \kappa = \frac{FOV}{FOV_{max}} \le 1, \qquad
    \eta = \frac{M_{cam}\,D_{proj}}{D_{cam}} \le 1.
    \label{eq:app-fractions}
\end{equation}
The first holds because the views must not overlap (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fov-mla-f}), and the second because the pupil image must fit on the sensor. The part of the lenslet pitch on the sensor that the image of the field leaves empty is a guard band.

To obtain $\alpha$ from them, note that by the definitions of $\kappa$ and $FOV_{max}$, the image of the field in the focal plane of a lenslet, $FOV\,M\,f_{MLA}/f_{FL}$, equals $\kappa\,d_{MLA}$, so the field spans $\kappa\,d_{MLA}M_{cam}$ on the camera. With $M_{cam}=\eta D_{cam}/D_{proj}$ and $D_{cam}=N_{px}P$, it covers $N_{field}=\kappa\,\eta\,N_{px}\,d_{MLA}/D_{proj}$ pixels. The field is $FOV=N_{field}\,p$ wide, and $D_{proj}/d_{MLA}=NA/NA_{view}=\Delta x_{LF}/\Delta x_o$, so
\begin{equation}
    \alpha = \frac{\Delta x_{LF}\,N_{field}}{FOV}
        = \eta\,\kappa\,\frac{N_{px}\,\Delta x_o}{FOV}
        \le \frac{N_{px}\,\Delta x_o}{FOV}.
    \label{eq:app-sampling}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is the sampling of a conventional microscope that images the same field onto the same camera, and equality holds when the field equals $FOV_{max}$ and the pupil image fills the sensor. Neither $N$ nor $NA_{view}$ appears in it: the light field keeps the pixel count of the conventional microscope and divides it among the views, each view coarser in resolution and in pixel count by the same factor.

In our instrument ($M=50$, $f_{FL}=750$~mm, $d_{MLA}=3$~mm, $f_{MLA}=55$~mm), $FOV_{max}\approx818~\mu$m, so the 550~$\mu$m field fills $\kappa\approx0.67$ of it. The camera relay ($M_{cam}=0.375$) maps the 22.5~mm pupil image onto 8.4~mm of the 12.6~mm sensor height ($N_{px}=4600$ pixels of 2.74~$\mu$m), so $\eta\approx0.67$. Then $M_T\approx1.38$, $p\approx1.99~\mu$m and, with $\Delta x_{LF}=2.58~\mu$m (\cref{sec:app-resolution}), $\alpha\approx1.29$, inside the targeted range. The bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-sampling} is about 2.9.

\paragraph{Undersampling.} If $\alpha<2$ the pixel, not the optics, limits the lateral resolution, which becomes about $2p$ instead of $\Delta x_{LF}$, a factor $2/\alpha$ coarser. The axial resolution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:axial-lf} is computed from the lateral one, so it worsens by the same factor. For our instrument, $2p\approx4~\mu$m against the optical $\Delta x_{LF}=2.58~\mu$m quoted in \cref{sec:design-parameters}.

\section{Resolution of a view}
\label{sec:app-resolution}

\paragraph{Through the focal lengths.} \Cref{sec:factors-of-n} treats each view as a wide-field microscope of aperture $NA_{view}$. The same lateral resolution follows from the lenslet. A lenslet of pitch $d_{MLA}$ and focal length $f_{MLA}$ has $NA_{MLA}\approx d_{MLA}/(2f_{MLA})$ and resolves $\lambda/(2NA_{MLA})$ in its focal plane. The Fourier lens and the lenslet image the intermediate image onto that plane with magnification $f_{MLA}/f_{FL}$, and the objective and tube lens add $M$, so in object space
\begin{equation}
    \Delta x_{LF} = \frac{\lambda}{2\,NA_{MLA}}\,\frac{f_{FL}}{f_{MLA}\,M}
    = \frac{\lambda\,f_{FL}}{M\,d_{MLA}}
    = \frac{\lambda}{2\,NA_{view}}, \qquad
    NA_{view} = \frac{M\,d_{MLA}}{2\,f_{FL}}.
    \label{eq:app-lateral-focal}
\end{equation}
The camera relay after the lenslets does not change this optical limit; the pixels enter only through $\alpha$ (\cref{sec:app-sampling}).

\paragraph{Whole views across the pupil.} The relations of \cref{sec:factors-of-n} assume that the pupil image holds exactly $N$ lenslets, $D_{proj}=N\,d_{MLA}$, so that $NA_{view}=NA/N$. In general the ratio $D_{proj}/d_{MLA}=NA/NA_{view}$ is not an integer. With the array centered on the optical axis and $N$ odd, the central lenslet and the $(N-1)/2$ lenslets on each side of it lie whole inside the pupil image when
\begin{equation}
    N \le \frac{D_{proj}}{d_{MLA}} < N+2
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \frac{NA}{N+2} < NA_{view} \le \frac{NA}{N},
    \label{eq:app-whole-views}
\end{equation}
and the light beyond them falls on partial lenslets whose views are not used. Our 22.5~mm pupil image and 3~mm pitch give a ratio of 7.5: seven whole views across at $NA_{view}=0.1$, with a quarter pitch left unused at each edge. \Cref{tab:app-ideal-fill} compares the values of Chapter~\ref{ch:light-field-microscope} for $N=7$ with those of the instrument.

With $\sin\theta_{max}\approx NA$, the axial resolution depends on the fill only through $\Delta x_{LF}$, and the ratio of depth of field to axial resolution is $NA/NA_{view}$: $N$ for the ideal fill, 7.5 for our instrument.

\paragraph{The largest viewing angle.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:axial-lf} takes the largest angle at the edge of the pupil, $\sin\theta_{max}\approx NA=N\,NA_{view}$, since the system accepts rays up to the objective's aperture. Following Guo \cite{guo2019fourierLightField}, one may instead take the central ray of the outermost view. Its lenslet lies $d_{max}=(N-1)\,d_{MLA}/2$ from the axis and views the intermediate image at $\tan\theta'=d_{max}/f_{FL}$, so a defocus $\Delta z_{im}$ there shifts the image by $\Delta z_{im}\,d_{max}/f_{FL}$. Setting this shift equal to the resolution $M\,\Delta x_{LF}$ in that plane, and returning to object space with the axial magnification $M^2$,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta z_{LF}' = \frac{\Delta x_{LF}\,f_{FL}}{M\,d_{max}}
    = \frac{\Delta x_{LF}}{(N-1)\,NA_{view}}
    = \frac{N}{N-1}\,\Delta z_{LF}.
    \label{eq:app-axial-dmax}
\end{equation}
The two forms differ by $N/(N-1)$, about $1+1/N$, and give $N$ and $N-1$ separable planes. For the ideal fill with $N=7$ the pupil-edge form gives 3.20~$\mu$m and the central-ray form 3.74~$\mu$m. Without the paraxial step ($\tan\theta$ in place of $\sin\theta$), the same rays give 2.12~$\mu$m at the pupil edge and 2.86~$\mu$m at the central ray of the outermost view. Both paraxial forms are therefore conservative, and the pupil-edge form is the closer estimate of what the outermost view measures (12\% above it, against 31\%).

\section{Depth of field}
\label{sec:app-dof}

The depth of field of a view is the smaller of two limits, the blur of the view itself and the cross-talk between neighboring views \cite{guo2019fourierLightField}.

\paragraph{Diffraction and pixel blur.} A view has aperture $NA_{view}$, so an emitter at defocus $z$ is blurred over a diameter of about $2\,z\,NA_{view}$ in object space. The blur reaches the diffraction distance $\Delta x_{LF}=\lambda/(2NA_{view})$ at $|z|=\lambda/(4NA_{view}^2)$ and the projected pixel $p$ at $|z|=p/(2NA_{view})$. Taking both sides of focus and adding the circle-of-confusion term to the diffraction term, as in the classical expression for a microscope, gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}^{px}
    = \frac{\lambda}{2\,NA_{view}^2} + \frac{p}{NA_{view}}
    = \mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right),
    \label{eq:app-dof-pixel}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:dof-lf} and the last step uses $p=\Delta x_{LF}/\alpha$ and $\mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}=\Delta x_{LF}/NA_{view}$. The pixel term is the fraction $1/(\alpha+1)$ of the total.

Guo's wave term is the full width at half maximum, twice the width used here, so in that convention the bracket is $(2+1/\alpha)$ and the pixel share is $1/(2\alpha+1)$:
\begin{equation}
    \text{pixel share} = \frac{1}{\alpha+1}\ \ (\text{this thesis}), \qquad
    \frac{1}{2\alpha+1}\ \ (\text{Guo}).
    \label{eq:app-dof-share}
\end{equation}
The pixel term is therefore not negligible anywhere in the targeted range $1\le\alpha\le2$: it is 33--50\% of the depth of field in this convention and 20--33\% in Guo's. At our $\alpha\approx1.29$ it is 44\% and 28\%, and it raises the depth of field of a view from 25.8~$\mu$m to about 46~$\mu$m. Chapter~\ref{ch:light-field-microscope} quotes the diffraction term alone, $\mathrm{Range}_{z,LF}$; the thesis does not measure the depth of field, and no result depends on its exact value.

\paragraph{Cross-talk.} An emitter off the focal plane shifts laterally in each off-axis view by the parallax of Section~\ref{sec:factors-of-n}. A view records only a window of the image formed by the objective and tube lens, of width $M\,FOV_{max}=d_{MLA}f_{FL}/f_{MLA}$, and an emitter in focus sits at its center, so it can shift by $M\,FOV_{max}/2$ there before its light enters the neighboring window (\cref{fig:app-crosstalk}). Following Guo we take the nearest lenslet, which views the emitter at $\tan\theta_1=d_{MLA}/f_{FL}$, and project the allowed range back to object space with the axial magnification $M^2$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Range}_{z}^{xt}
    = \frac{1}{M^2}\,\frac{M\,FOV_{max}}{\tan\theta_1}
    = \frac{f_{FL}^2}{M^2\,f_{MLA}}.
    \label{eq:app-dof-crosstalk}
\end{equation}
This is purely geometric: the pitch cancels, and neither the wavelength nor the pixel size enters. For our design values ($f_{FL}=750$~mm, $f_{MLA}=55$~mm, $M=50$) it is about 4~mm, far above Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-dof-pixel}, so the cross-talk limit does not bind.
